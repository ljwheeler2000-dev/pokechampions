# Team Build: Mega Froslass Sweeper + Aurora Veil Support

Built via the 7-step methodology (claude/teambuilding-methodology.md), Reg M-B doubles.

## Concept

Mega Froslass (Ice/Ghost, Snow Warning) as a dedicated sweeper — freed from screen duty so it
can run a full attacking set (Life Orb / Choice item / physical Ice Spinner) rather than
splitting its moveslot and item on Aurora Veil itself. A second Ice-type carries Light Clay
and sets Aurora Veil instead.

**Aurora Veil learnset is restricted in this game** — confirmed via in-game screenshot: only
Alolan Ninetales, Abomasnow/Mega Abomasnow, Froslass/Mega Froslass, Vanilluxe, Aurorus, and
Avalugg/Hisuian Avalugg can learn it. Every option is Ice-type, so the setter unavoidably
shares Froslass's Fire and Rock weaknesses — a structural tradeoff of the archetype.

## Roster (6/6 locked)

1. **Mega Froslass** (Ice/Ghost) — sweeper. Weak to Fire, Rock, Ghost, Dark, Steel (all 2x);
   immune to Normal and Fighting; resists Ice, Poison, Bug.
2. **Alolan Ninetales** (Ice/Fairy) — Aurora Veil + Light Clay setter. Full weakness profile:
   Steel (4x), Fire, Poison, Rock (2x); immune Dragon; resists Ice, Bug, Dark.
3. **Incineroar** (Fire/Dark) — resists Fire, Grass, Ice, Ghost, Dark, Steel; immune Psychic;
   weak to Water, Fighting, Ground, Rock (all 2x). Brings Fake Out and Intimidate.
4. **Kingambit** (Dark/Steel) — weak to Fire, Fighting (4x), Ground. Resists Rock, Dark,
   Steel, Ghost, Ice, Grass, Normal, Flying, Dragon; immune Poison and Psychic. Always holds
   **Chople Berry** (halves the first super-effective Fighting hit it takes).
5. **Sneasler** (Fighting/Poison) — replaced Kommo-o (not available in the owner's roster). Weak
   to Ground, Flying (2x), Psychic (4x); immune Ghost; resists Grass, Fighting, Poison, Bug
   (¼x), Rock, Dark; neutral to Fire, Steel, Fairy.
6. **Milotic** (Water) — resists Fire and Steel, neutral to Rock. Weak to Grass and Electric
   (2x) — its only two weaknesses. No new weaknesses shared with any other locked member.
   Also the anchor of the separate Tyranitar core team
   (claude/team-tyranitar-sinistcha-milotic.md) — reused here with no conflict. Picked as the
   bulkier, more defensive option over Basculegion specifically because the team needs
   something that can repeatedly switch into Fire attacks and survive, not just resist one hit.

## The Kommo-o → Sneasler swap: what changed

Kommo-o resisted both Fire **and** Rock at once — that was the specific reason it was picked
for this slot. Sneasler only resists Rock; it's neutral to Fire, so Fire remains a clean
3-member weakness (Froslass, Ninetales, Kingambit) with no added resist from this slot —
Milotic and Incineroar remain the team's only two Fire answers, unchanged from before the swap.

**What improved:** Kommo-o's 4x Fairy weakness — the team's single scariest liability given
Sylveon's S-tier status — is gone. Sneasler is neutral to Fairy, not weak to it. Nobody on the
team resists Fairy, but nobody is catastrophically exposed to it either anymore.

**What got worse:** Sneasler's Ground weakness stacks onto Incineroar and Kingambit's existing
overlap — Ground is now a 3-member weakness (was 2). Isolated new weaknesses to Flying and
Psychic (4x) as well, shared with nothing else on the team.

Net: a fair trade, not a clean upgrade — better Fairy matchup and same Rock coverage, at the
cost of losing the Fire resist and worsening the Ground overlap.

## Final defensive picture (post-swap)

- **Fire — 3 of 6 weak** (Froslass, Ninetales, Kingambit); Incineroar and Milotic resist.
- **Rock — 3 of 6 weak** (Froslass, Ninetales, Incineroar); Kingambit and Sneasler resist.
- **Steel — 2 of 6 weak** (Froslass, Ninetales 4x); Incineroar, Kingambit, Milotic resist.
- **Ground — 3 of 6 weak** (Incineroar, Kingambit, Sneasler) — new since the swap.
- **Fairy — 0 of 6 resist, but 0 of 6 catastrophically weak** (all neutral) — improved since
  the swap; previously Kommo-o was 4x weak.

## Movesets (locked)

1. **Mega Froslass:** Blizzard / Shadow Ball / Aurora Veil / Protect
2. **Alolan Ninetales:** Aurora Veil / Protect / Blizzard / Moonblast
3. **Incineroar:** Fake Out / Flare Blitz / Darkest Lariat / Parting Shot
4. **Kingambit:** Sucker Punch / Kowtow Cleave / Iron Head / Protect — item: Chople Berry
5. **Sneasler:** Close Combat / Dire Claw / Fake Out / Protect — gives the team a **second
   Fake Out user** alongside Incineroar, enabling a double-Fake-Out turn 1 against dangerous
   fast leads (Aerodactyl, Mega Gengar, etc.) at the cost of no damage that turn. Close Combat
   drops Sneasler's own Def/SpDef, which compounds a bit with its Ground/Flying/Psychic
   weaknesses if it takes a follow-up hit — standard tradeoff for the set, usually offset by
   Unburden-style speed if that's the ability run.
6. **Milotic:** Scald / Protect / Icy Wind / Recover

## Known counters to watch for (per the Game8 Snow Teams guide)

- **Fast/strong answers:** Mega Gengar, Aerodactyl, Greninja. (Mega Gengar is on the separate
  Tyranitar core team — a mirror-matchup note if the owner ever plays both.) Aerodactyl was
  considered and passed on for this team specifically because, despite resisting Fire, its own
  Rock weakness would have worsened the team's biggest existing problem.
- **Weather override:** Torkoal, Politoed, Tyranitar, Mega Charizard-Y.
- **Sylveon** (S-tier) — less catastrophic post-swap (no more 4x target) but still a threat
  given zero resists anywhere on the team.
- **Kingambit (S-tier, opposing)** and **Archaludon (A-tier)** — both hit Alolan Ninetales at
  4x via Steel STAB (Iron Head / Steel moves). The team's own Kingambit doesn't help against
  an opposing one. **Confirmed unsurvivable by bulk investment** (see below) — Ninetales must
  be played around these two positionally, not statted around.
- **Charizard-Mega-Y (S-tier)** — 2x Fire STAB into Ninetales/Froslass/Kingambit.

### Incineroar-specific meta threats (by weakness)

Incineroar's four 2x weaknesses (Water, Fighting, Ground, Rock) map onto real S/A-tier and
sand-core threats:

- **Water:** Basculegion (S-tier, Adaptability Wave Crash/Liquidation), Pelipper (S-tier, rain
  Weather Ball/Scald/Hydro Pump), Mega Swampert (rain Liquidation), Mega Starmie (Liquidation).
- **Ground:** Garchomp (S-tier, Earthquake), Excadrill (sand core staple, High Horsepower/
  Earthquake, often Sand Rush-boosted).
- **Rock:** Tyranitar (mega or regular, Stone Edge — sand core staple), Aerodactyl-Mega (ran on
  the largest-tournament winning team alongside Sneasler and Kingambit); Rock Slide is also a
  common doubles spread move, so this is a "gets hit even when not targeted" risk.
- **Fighting:** an opposing Sneasler's Close Combat. (Archaludon was previously listed here via
  Body Press — **corrected: Body Press is not learnable by Archaludon in this game.** Without
  it, Archaludon's typical kit — Electro Shot/Draco Meteor/Flash Cannon — is neutral-to-resisted
  against Incineroar, so it's not actually a Fighting-weakness threat here.)
- **Notable despite resist:** Kingambit is the #1 mon in the format (S-tier). Incineroar
  resists its Dark STAB, but Supreme Overlord scaling + Swords Dance can still push a resisted
  hit into KO range — "resisted" isn't the same as "safe" here.

### Milotic-specific meta threats (by weakness)

Milotic's two 2x weaknesses (Grass, Electric) are narrower than the rest of the team's, but
both are directly covered by named S/A-tier threats:

- **Grass:** Sinistcha (A-tier, Matcha Gotcha — also the Grass piece of the sand core) and
  Venusaur-Mega (A-tier, highest win rate in the whole A tier at 57.6%, Leaf Storm).
- **Electric:** Archaludon (A-tier, Electro Shot — instant-cast and rain-boosted whenever
  Pelipper's S-tier rain is up) and Raichu (Zap Cannon).
- **Not a weakness but a trap matchup:** Charizard-Mega-Y (S-tier) commonly carries Solar Beam
  specifically to answer bulky Water walls like Milotic — flips what looks like a safe
  Fire-resist switch-in into a 2x Grass hit instead.

### Kingambit-specific meta threats (by weakness)

Kingambit is weak to Fire (2x), Fighting (**4x**, mitigated once per game by Chople Berry),
and Ground (2x); immune to Poison and Psychic; resists everything else.

- **Fighting (4x, softened by Chople):** **Mega Staraptor** (S-tier, #4 overall) is Fighting/
  Flying per this project's confirmed data, and its Champions-exclusive Contrary ability pairs
  well with Close Combat (Contrary flips the stat drop into a gain) — a likely, not confirmed,
  set. An opposing **Sneasler**'s Close Combat is the more straightforward 4x threat. Both are
  now calc'd (see spread below) — Chople Berry plus screens is what actually makes these
  survivable; without screens, Kingambit's low Def investment does not clear either breakpoint.
- **Fire (2x):** Charizard-Mega-Y (S-tier, Heat Wave and, under sun, a Weather Ball that stays
  unsurvivable even with screens up), Mega Delphox (Heat Wave — safe outside of sun, but
  unsurvivable without screens once sun is active from any source), Torkoal (sun Eruption —
  survivable with screens and minimal investment), Incineroar (Flare Blitz — cheaply survived
  even without screens, see spread below).
- **Ground (2x):** Garchomp (S-tier), Excadrill (sand core) — per the owner's testing, "the ground
  moves don't interact a ton" against Kingambit's natural bulk, so no dedicated breakpoint was
  needed here.
- **Notable non-threats worth naming:** Basculegion, Sylveon, Farigiraf, and Pelipper are all
  neutral-or-resisted into Kingambit by typing — none of the other S-tier mons exploit its
  weaknesses directly.

## Verified Speed SP spreads (Froslass, Ninetales, Sneasler — via the owner's pokechampions calc)

- **Mega Froslass: 171 Speed.** Reached *without* a positive Speed nature — base Speed is high
  enough on its own. This freed the nature slot for **+SpA instead**, so Froslass gets both the
  Speed benchmark and a boosted Special Attack. Remaining SP after maxing Speed and SpA: only
  **3 HP** (higher raw SP cost for Speed without the 10% nature assist leaves less remainder).
- **Alolan Ninetales: 170 Speed.** Needed a **+Speed nature** to hit this, leaving SpA neutral
  (still maxed at 32 SP, just no nature bonus). Remainder: **8 HP**.
- **Sneasler: 179 Speed.** Also needed a **+Speed nature**, Attack maxed at 32 SP neutral gain.
  Remainder: **11 HP**.
- Both Froslass and Ninetales's 171/170 clear a max-SP, positive-nature Garchomp (a benchmark
  the owner tuned against directly).
- Sneasler's 179 clears Hisuian Zoroark, Mega Staraptor, Mega Metagross, and opposing Alolan
  Ninetales at their maximum Speed investment. **Checked against this project's own confirmed
  data:** Mega Staraptor's base Speed (110, from claude/damage-calculator-guide.md) gives
  floor((110+20+32)×1.1) = **178** at max SP/nature — Sneasler's 179 clears it by exactly 1
  point, a deliberately tuned breakpoint, not a coincidence. (The Zoroark/Metagross/Ninetales
  benchmarks aren't independently checked here — no confirmed base stats for those in the
  project docs yet, so they're taken as accurate from the owner's own calc rather than re-derived.)

**Important distinction:** beating Garchomp/Staraptor/etc. on Speed means those three act
first against those specific threats, which is real value — but it does **not** resolve the
separately-flagged Ninetales bulk problem (opposing Kingambit/Archaludon 4x Iron Head still
lands regardless of who's faster; Speed doesn't reduce that damage).

## Ninetales vs. opposing Kingambit/Archaludon: resolved as a positional problem, not a stat one

The owner confirmed via his own calc: **no amount of bulk investment survives an opposing Iron
Head or Flash Cannon** at 4x effectiveness from these two. This isn't a spread to keep chasing
— within the 66 SP budget there's no breakpoint that saves Ninetales from that hit. The answer
is play pattern, not itemization: keep Ninetales out of Iron Head/Flash Cannon range, Protect
on a predicted turn, and treat an opposing Kingambit or Archaludon as a hard "don't lead
Ninetales into this" read at team preview.

## Incineroar SP spread (verified via the owner's calc)

**Breakpoints found, without Aurora Veil up:**
- Basculegion Adaptability Wave Crash: **unsurvivable at any SP** (always OHKO).
- Pelipper Hydro Pump: **unsurvivable at any SP** (always OHKO). Muddy Water always survived
  regardless.
- Mega Swampert Liquidation in rain: **unsurvivable at any SP**. Without rain: survives at
  **16 Def** + max HP.
- Mega Starmie Liquidation: **unsurvivable at any SP**, rain or not.
- Excadrill High Horsepower: survives at **20 Def**.
- Mega Tyranitar Stone Edge: **unsurvivable at any SP**. Regular (non-Mega) Tyranitar Stone
  Edge: survives at **27 Def**.

**Same breakpoints, with Aurora Veil up** (only these three were retested under Veil):
- Mega Swampert rain Liquidation: survives at **16 Def** (down from unsurvivable).
- Pelipper Hydro Pump: survives at **9 SpDef** (down from unsurvivable).
- Mega Tyranitar Stone Edge: survives at **0 SP** (down from unsurvivable) — i.e. always
  survives once Veil is up, regardless of investment.

**Internal consistency check:** rain's 1.5x damage boost and Aurora Veil's ~0.667x reduction
roughly cancel (1.5 × 0.667 ≈ 1.0), which is exactly why the no-veil/non-rain Swampert
breakpoint (16 Def) matches the with-veil/rain Swampert breakpoint (16 Def) — strong signal
the two data sets are internally consistent rather than one being a fluke.

**Locked spread: 16 Def / 9 SpDef / 32 HP (max) / 9 Atk / 0 SpA / 0 Speed.** Totals exactly
**66/66** with no waste. Speed is intentionally 0 — Incineroar's job here is Fake Out +
Intimidate + Parting Shot support and a Flare Blitz/Darkest Lariat attacker, not a speed-tier
racer, and the team already controls speed via Froslass/Ninetales/Sneasler.

**Known residual risk — this spread is built around Veil being up.** 16 Def clears the
Swampert/Mega-TTar cases *with Veil*, but two tested no-veil breakpoints sit above 16 Def and
were never retested under Veil: Excadrill High Horsepower (needs 20 Def without Veil) and
regular Tyranitar Stone Edge (needs 27 Def without Veil — likely moot in practice since the
documented sand core runs the Mega, not the regular, Tyranitar). Garchomp Earthquake was never
calc'd at all. Practical exposure window: turn 1 before Ninetales/Froslass has set Veil, and
any turn after Veil expires in a longer game. Treat this the same way as the Ninetales
Kingambit/Archaludon problem — a play-pattern note (don't let Incineroar eat an Excadrill hit
before Veil is up) rather than a reason to re-budget SP, unless the owner wants to calc the
Veil-up numbers for Excadrill/Garchomp to close the gap.

## Milotic SP spread (verified via the owner's calc)

**Breakpoints found (all Special attacks checked against SpDef unless noted):**
- Solar Beam: without screens, **always 2HKOs regardless of SP** — no breakpoint clears it.
  With screens: **21 SpDef** prevents the 2HKO (pushes to 3HKO+).
- Electro Shot (Archaludon): **24 SpDef** prevents the OHKO, screens or not — but it's still
  always a 2HKO at that point regardless of screens.
- Raichu Zap Cannon: without screens, **always OHKOs regardless of SP** (unsurvivable, same
  category as Incineroar's Basculegion/Starmie problem). With screens: always just a 2HKO,
  never an OHKO, regardless of SP.
- Matcha Gotcha (Sinistcha): without screens, **9 SpDef** prevents the 2HKO. With screens:
  always just a 4HKO regardless of investment — the least dangerous of the four special hits.
- Leaf Storm (Mega Venusaur): without screens, **21 SpDef** prevents the OHKO. With screens:
  no SP needed — always just a 2HKO regardless of investment.
- Last Respects (Basculegion, physical — checked against Def, not SpDef): **7 Def** survives
  even a maxed-out hit.

**Locked spread: 32 HP (max) / 7 Def / 18 SpA / 9 SpDef with a Calm (+SpDef) nature / 0 Atk /
0 Speed.** Totals exactly **66/66**.

**Verification — the nature math checks out exactly.** 9 SP with the +10% Calm nature produces
a final SpDef stat of **169**, which is precisely the stat value that 24 raw SpDef (no nature)
would produce — i.e. this spread reaches the Electro Shot no-OHKO breakpoint (the single
hardest one tested) using only 9 SP instead of 24, freeing the other 15 SP for Special Attack.
Backing Milotic's base SpDef out of these numbers gives **125**, which matches Milotic's known
base SpDef from the mainline games — independent confirmation this is computed correctly, not
just internally consistent. The same 169 stat comfortably clears the 21-SpDef Solar
Beam/Leaf Storm breakpoints and the 9-SpDef Matcha Gotcha breakpoint too.

**Known residual risk:** Raichu's Zap Cannon is an unconditional OHKO without screens up, and
Solar Beam's no-screens 2HKO can't be pushed further by any amount of investment — both fall
into the same "unfixable by SP, only by play pattern" bucket as Incineroar's Basculegion/
Mega Starmie problem and Ninetales's Kingambit/Archaludon problem. This spread's safety is
Veil-uptime-dependent, consistent with the rest of the team's defensive plan.

## Kingambit SP spread (verified via the owner's calc)

**Breakpoints found** (all tested with Chople Berry active on the first Fighting hit):
- Sneasler Close Combat: without screens, **16 Def** + max HP stops the OHKO. With screens:
  always survives the OHKO regardless of SP.
- Mega Staraptor (likely Close Combat): without screens, **24 Def** needed. With screens:
  always survives regardless of SP.
- Incineroar Flare Blitz: without screens, **3 Def** stops the OHKO.
- Charizard-Mega-Y Heat Wave: without screens, **always OHKOs regardless of SP**. With
  screens: **1 SpDef** stops the OHKO.
- Charizard-Mega-Y Weather Ball (sun-boosted): **unsurvivable even with screens**, at any SP.
- Mega Delphox Heat Wave: safe by default outside of sun with no investment needed; **once sun
  is active from any source, unsurvivable without screens**, at any SP.
- Torkoal sun Eruption: survives with screens and **3 SpDef**.
- Ground moves (Garchomp/Excadrill): "don't interact a ton" — no dedicated breakpoint needed
  against Kingambit's natural bulk.

**Locked spread: 32 HP (max) / 28 Attack (with a nature boost) / 3 Def / 3 SpDef / 0 SpA /
0 Speed.** Totals exactly **66/66**. Def and SpDef are set to the cheapest breakpoints that
clear a real no-screens threat (Incineroar's Flare Blitz, and the higher of Torkoal's/Zard-Y's
screened SpDef needs) — everything else left over goes to Attack, leaning into Kingambit as
a genuine attacker rather than a pure wall.

**Deliberate tradeoff, stated by the owner: "lean into offense, and get off the field without
screens against some of these Pokémon."** At only 3 Def, Kingambit does **not** clear the
Sneasler (16) or Mega Staraptor (24) Close Combat breakpoints without screens — it will go
down to either one if Veil isn't up. Screens make both fully safe regardless of SP (Chople
Berry does the rest), so — same as Ninetales, Incineroar, and Milotic — Kingambit's survival
against its two biggest single threats is Veil-uptime-dependent by design, not an oversight.
Weather Ball and sun-boosted Delphox Heat Wave remain unconditional losses without screens no
matter the spread — accepted, same "positional not statistical" bucket as the team's other
unfixable matchups.
