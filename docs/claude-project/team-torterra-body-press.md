# Team Build: Torterra Body Press (Bulky Priority Control)

Built via the 7-step methodology (claude/teambuilding-methodology.md), Reg M-B doubles.
**Status: 6-mon roster locked — Torterra / Incineroar / Milotic / Archaludon (locked 4) +
Mega Froslass / Tyranitar as a flex Mega pair, chosen at team preview per matchup (snow vs.
sand). Grimmsnarl cut to make room — confirmed. Tyranitar: Crunch / Rock Slide / Protect
confirmed, 4th move still being weighed between Fire Punch (current lean) and Brick Break
— see Tyranitar section for why the Kingambit rationale for Brick Break didn't hold up.
Remaining opens: Tyranitar 4th move + item, accepted zero-screens-on-sand-days trade-off,
Dragon Pulse vs. Electro Shot on Archaludon, Wide Guard verification, Ground-answer
verification.**

## Step 1-2: Format and idea

Reg M-B doubles (see claude/meta-report-reg-mb-week8.md). Meta is dominated by Rain
(Pelipper/Archaludon/Venusaur-Mega/Grimmsnarl/Basculegion/Charizard-Mega-Y) and Tailwind
(Charizard-Mega-Y/Sylveon/Farigiraf), both speed-control archetypes. Sand
(Excadrill/Tyranitar-Mega/Sinistcha) is most-played but slipping. Pelipper and Sylveon both
moved into S tier this window.

**The philosophy (as stated): "sit here and eat your attack to the face, then KO you in
return regardless of speed."** Bulky control archetype, not Trick Room or Tailwind.
**Actual speed-control tool ended up being Icy Wind spam** (Mega Froslass + Milotic both
run it, on snow-game-plan days), not pure priority as originally planned.

**The Body Press idea:** Body Press deals damage off the user's **Defense** stat instead
of Attack — note the move's *type* is Fighting (this matters for Kingambit, see Tyranitar
section below). Iron Defense (+2 Defense per use) buffs the move's damage and Torterra's
own bulk on the same turn.

**Ability: Shell Armor** (hidden), not Overgrow — protects the stacked Defense investment
from crits.

**Torterra typing (Grass/Ground), per claude/type-chart.md:**
- Immune: Electric
- 4x weak: Ice
- 2x weak: Fire, Flying, Bug
- Resists (0.5x): Ground, Rock

## Core weaknesses/gaps (for reference)
- **Fire** hits 2 of the original 4 core members for 2x (Torterra, Mega Froslass) — answered
  by Milotic's Water STAB (2x back), and resisted directly by Tyranitar on sand days.
- **Rock** hits 2 of the original 4 core members for 2x (Incineroar, Mega Froslass) —
  answered by Milotic's Water STAB, and resisted directly by Archaludon.
- **Fairy** was the one true offensive gap (nothing on Torterra/Incineroar/Froslass/Milotic
  hits it) — Sylveon is S-tier (Tailwind), mono-typed, resists Incineroar's Dark. This
  project's chart also has Fairy hit Ghost for 2x (not neutral like mainline), so Mega
  Froslass is itself weak to Sylveon and was never a candidate to answer it.
- Milotic's Sludge Bomb plan died — **not in its movepool, confirmed**. Fairy answer moved
  to the 6th slot instead.

## Archaludon (Steel/Dragon), item White Herb — confirmed, locked core member

Chosen specifically to answer Sylveon (Flash Cannon, 2x) after Milotic's coverage plan
fell through. Turned out to fit the team well beyond that one job:
- **Resists Rock** (Steel ½ × Dragon 1 = ½) — directly patches one of the two shared core
  weaknesses.
- **Neutral to Fire** (Steel 2 × Dragon ½ = 1) — doesn't add to the other shared weakness.
- **Second Protect user** on the team (Milotic has the first).
- Independently strong in the actual meta (A-tier, 50.1% WR per the meta report) — not a
  synergy-only pick.

**Caveats on the Sylveon matchup specifically:**
- Only Flash Cannon actually hits Sylveon — Draco Meteor and Dragon Pulse are both flat 0x
  vs. Fairy (Dragon-type moves are always immune to Fairy).
- Archaludon does **not** get a defensive resistance to Fairy despite being part Steel —
  Dragon's own 2x Fairy weakness cancels Steel's 0.5x resist, netting neutral. Only the
  offensive side (Flash Cannon) does the job.
- Draco Meteor and Flash Cannon are each resisted (½x) by Kingambit — not a problem, since
  Torterra and Incineroar already handle Kingambit well (see Tyranitar section below).

**New individual weaknesses (don't compound the team's Fire/Rock problem, but do overlap
with Tyranitar — see below):** 2x weak to Ground, 2x weak to Fighting.

**Open moveset question — Dragon Pulse vs. Electro Shot:** White Herb + Draco Meteor is a
sound standard pairing. Dragon Pulse as the 3rd move is redundant with Draco Meteor (same
typing, weaker, and doesn't escape the post-White-Herb SpA drop any better than any other
special move would). **Suggested swap: Electro Shot.** Correction from earlier in this
build: Electric hits Flying for 2x in this project's chart, same as mainline (earlier
statement that Flying resists Electric was wrong) — so Electro Shot vs. Pelipper
(Water/Flying, also newly S-tier) is a clean **4x**, and nothing else on the team currently
touches Pelipper. Not yet confirmed which the user wants to run.

## Confirmed movesets

- **Torterra:** Body Press / Wood Hammer / Iron Defense / Synthesis. No Protect, no
  Stealth Rock, no Earthquake. Body Press is Fighting-type — this is the team's actual
  answer to Kingambit (4x, Fighting hits both Dark and Steel for 2x each).
- **Incineroar:** Flare Blitz / Darkest Lariat / Parting Shot / Fake Out. No Protect. Flare
  Blitz adds a second Kingambit answer (2x, Fire vs. Steel) — Darkest Lariat is resisted
  (½, Dark resists itself).
- **Mega Froslass (snow days):** Aurora Veil / Blizzard / Shadow Ball / Icy Wind. **Sets
  its own Snow (confirmed)** — self-sufficient when it's the active Mega. Snow-setting
  appears to be tied to the Mega itself (not yet verified) — on sand days, when Tyranitar
  is brought instead, Froslass isn't on the team at all, so this doesn't come up.
- **Milotic:** Scald / Recover / Icy Wind / Protect. Icy Wind is redundant with Mega
  Froslass's copy on snow days — worth revisiting later, not changed for now.
- **Archaludon:** Draco Meteor / Dragon Pulse (pending Electro Shot swap) / Flash Cannon /
  Protect. Item: White Herb.
- **Tyranitar (sand days):** Crunch / Rock Slide / Protect confirmed. **4th move: still
  being weighed** between Fire Punch (current lean) and Brick Break — see Tyranitar
  section below. Item still to be decided.

## Grimmsnarl cut — confirmed, replaced by the Tyranitar flex pair

**Decision made:** cut Grimmsnarl (not Torterra) to make room for Tyranitar. Reasoning
that led here:
- Torterra is the team's namesake and entire defensive-boost engine (Iron
  Defense/Body Press/Synthesis) — cutting it changes the team's identity, not just its
  roster.
- Grimmsnarl's Parting Shot and Fake Out were fully redundant with Incineroar's copies, so
  little unique offense/tempo was lost there.
- Psychic immunity (blocks Farigiraf's STAB) carries over cleanly — Tyranitar's Dark half
  blocks Psychic the same way Grimmsnarl's did.

**Accepted cost:** Grimmsnarl's Light Screen was the team's only screen source independent
of Mega Froslass. On snow days Aurora Veil already covers both screens for free (Grimmsnarl
was redundant there anyway). **On sand days, Froslass isn't brought at all, so there is no
Aurora Veil and — with Grimmsnarl gone — no screens whatsoever that game.** This was
surfaced explicitly and accepted as a trade-off for the matchup flexibility, not an
oversight.

## Tyranitar (Dark/Rock), sand-day Mega — typing analysis

**Defensive fit mirrors Archaludon's:** resists Fire (Fire ½ × Rock/Dark 1 = ½) and is
neutral to Rock (Rock 1 × Dark 1 = 1) — patches one of the team's two original shared
weaknesses (Fire) without adding to the other.

**But shares a new weak point with Archaludon:** 2x weak to Ground, and **4x weak to
Fighting** (Rock/Dark takes Fighting 2x on each half). Archaludon is already 2x weak to
both. On any sand-day game (Archaludon + Tyranitar both fielded), a single opposing Ground
or Fighting spread move threatens two of the team's four active Pokémon at once — and the
team still has no confirmed Wide Guard to blunt that. Tyranitar is also newly 2x weak to
Water (relevant: Basculegion, S-tier), Grass, Steel, and Bug.

**Not yet verified:** whether base (non-Mega) Tyranitar's Sand Stream is innate/independent
of Mega status the way it typically is in mainline — if so, sand can come up reliably
regardless of whether Tyranitar is the active Mega that turn, an asymmetry favoring it over
Froslass's apparently Mega-locked snow.

### Moveset: Crunch / Rock Slide / Protect confirmed, 4th slot still open

**Correction — Kingambit is not actually a gap the 4th move needs to fill.** Body Press is
Fighting-type (the move's damage calc uses Defense, but its type is Fighting), so Torterra
already hits Kingambit for 4x (Fighting hits both Dark and Steel for 2x each) whenever it's
on the field, and Incineroar's Flare Blitz adds another 2x. Brick Break's main selling
point — a "unique" 4x answer to Kingambit — was wrong; the team already has that, twice
over. Neither Crunch (½, Dark resists itself) nor Rock Slide (½, Steel resists Rock) touch
Kingambit, but that's fine — they don't need to.

**Current lean: Fire Punch over Brick Break**, on Ice coverage (both do this equally — the
sand lineup has no Ice-type move anywhere else since Froslass isn't in it) plus Venusaur-
Mega: Fire hits it for a clean 2x (Grass 2x, Poison neutral) where Brick Break is actually
resisted (Fighting is ½ vs. Poison) — and Venusaur-Mega has the single highest win rate of
anything in A-tier per the meta report (57.6%). Fire Punch also lets Tyranitar punish Grass
and Bug, two of its own weaknesses. **Not yet decided — still being weighed.**
